# MYP Mathematics 1 — Spec-gap additions (Try-it checks + Stretch & Apply)

The book's "How to use this coursebook" (p8) lists Definition, Key idea, Common error, ATL
and Criterion-strip boxes. Per the course spec, two more box types are required in every
chapter: **Try-it checks** (0 in the book) and **Stretch & Apply / SaA** boxes (0 in the
book). This file provides one of each per chapter, ready to drop in, with answers keyed to
the end-of-book answers section.

**Placement convention**
- **Try-it check**: immediately after the first worked example of the chapter's central
  concept (section noted per chapter below).
- **Stretch & Apply (SaA)**: at the end of the concept sequence, just before the chapter's
  investigation/application.

**Label-page text to add (p8, "How the labels work"):**
> Try-it checks ask one or two rapid questions the moment a method is first shown. They test
> whether the new move is safe before you carry on; answers are given, so you can correct
> yourself in seconds.
>
> Stretch & Apply boxes move the method one step into a new context. They are deliberately
> short, sit at the end of a concept sequence, and reward connecting the idea to something
> you have already seen.

## Chapter 1 · Number systems and place value
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 1.3 (expanded form); SaA before the
real-life application "Numbers for a community noticeboard".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Write 5,072 in expanded form.
2. In 3,006, what does the zero in the hundreds place tell you?
*Answers: (1) 5,000 + 70 + 2 (fully: 5×1,000 + 0×100 + 7×10 + 2×1). (2) The hundreds place
is empty; the zero holds the place so the 3 stays in the thousands and the 6 in the ones.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A ticket machine displays `030,004`. Removing the leading zero gives 3,004 — the value is
unchanged. Explain, using place value, why the machine still needs that zero.
*Answer direction: the zero fixes which column the 3 sits in (thousands, not hundreds);
fixed-width columns keep each digit's place value unambiguous — form, not value, is what
the zero carries.*

## Chapter 2 · Powers, expanded form and order of operations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 2.1 (powers); SaA before "The school
data store".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Work out 2⁶.
2. Write 64 as a power of 4.
*Answers: (1) 64. (2) 4³.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A class website doubles its storage each year, starting from 8 GB. Write a rule for the
storage S (GB) after n years and find S when n = 5.
*Answer: S = 8 × 2ⁿ; S(5) = 8 × 32 = 256 GB.*

## Chapter 3 · Factors, multiples and primes
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 3.2 (divisibility tests); SaA before
"The fairness relay".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Is 34,521 divisible by 3? By 9?
2. Without dividing, show that 2,468 is divisible by 4.
*Answers: (1) 3+4+5+2+1 = 15, so yes by 3, no by 9 (15 is not a multiple of 9).
(2) The last two digits give 68 = 4 × 17, so yes.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Two meshing gears have 12 and 18 teeth. How many teeth must pass the meshing point before
both gears are back in their starting orientation at the same time?
*Answer: LCM(12, 18) = 36 teeth (the 12-tooth gear has turned 3 times, the 18-tooth gear 2).*

## Chapter 4 · Fractions and decimals
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 4.1 (equivalent fractions); SaA before
"The scaling kitchen".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Simplify 18/24.
2. Write the fraction equivalent to 3/4 with denominator 20.
*Answers: (1) 3/4. (2) 15/20.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A recipe for 6 people needs 1/2 cup of sugar. A student scales it to 9 people and measures
3/4 cup. Check that the scaling is correct, then find the amount for 8 people.
*Answer: 1/2 × 9/6 = 3/4, so the student is right; for 8 people, 1/2 × 8/6 = 2/3 cup.*

## Chapter 5 · Percentages and applications
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 5.2 (percentage of a quantity); SaA
before "The community market stall".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find 15% of 240.
2. A shirt costs £36. Find its price after a 25% discount.
*Answers: (1) 36. (2) 25% of 36 = 9, so £27.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Shop A offers 50% off, then a further 50% off at the till. Shop B offers 60% off. On a £40
jacket, which shop is cheaper and by how much?
*Answer: A: £40 → £20 → £10; B: £40 − £24 = £16. Shop A is £6 cheaper — two halving
discounts beat a single 60% discount.*

## Chapter 6 · Patterns, expressions and equations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 6.1 (patterns and rules); SaA before
"The courtyard pond".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find a rule for the sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, … in terms of the term number n.
2. Use your rule to find the 10th term.
*Answers: (1) 4n − 1. (2) 4 × 10 − 1 = 39.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A chain of n squares made from matchsticks uses 3n + 1 matches. How many more matches does
the chain for 25 squares need than the chain for 12 squares?
*Answer: (3 × 25 + 1) − (3 × 12 + 1) = 76 − 37 = 39 — each added square costs exactly 3.*

## Chapter 7 · Lines, angles, triangles and constructions
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 7.1 (angle facts); SaA before "The
roof-truss workshop".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Two angles lie on a straight line; one is 118°. Find the other.
2. Three angles meet at a point; two are 140° and 95°. Find the third.
*Answers: (1) 62°. (2) 360 − 140 − 95 = 125°.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
At 4:00 on a clock, find the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand.
*Answer: the minute hand is at 12 (0°); the hour hand is 4 hours past 12, i.e. 4 × 30° =
120° from the 12 — the angle is 120°.*

## Chapter 8 · Sets and classification
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 8.2 (Venn diagrams); SaA before "The
centre audit".*

**TRY-IT**
Let A = {1, 2, 3, 4} and B = {3, 4, 5, 6}.
1. Find n(A ∩ B).
2. Write A ∪ B.
*Answers: (1) 2. (2) {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
In a survey of 30 students, 18 like tea, 15 like coffee and 7 like neither. How many like
both?
*Answer: 30 − 7 = 23 like at least one; 18 + 15 = 33 counts the "both" students twice, so
33 − 23 = 10.*

## Chapter 9 · Perimeter, area, surface area and volume
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 9.1 (area of triangles and
quadrilaterals); SaA before "The courtyard project".*

**TRY-IT**
1. A triangle has base 8 cm and height 5 cm. Find its area.
2. A 9 cm by 4 cm rectangle has the same area as a square. Find the square's side.
*Answers: (1) ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm². (2) 9 × 4 = 36 cm², so the side is 6 cm.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A rectangular tank, 20 cm by 15 cm, is filled to a depth of 8 cm. Find the volume of water
in millilitres (1 cm³ = 1 mL).
*Answer: 20 × 15 × 8 = 2,400 cm³ = 2,400 mL.*

## Chapter 10 · Data, tables and graphs
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 10.2 (bar and pie charts); SaA before
"The canteen survey".*

**TRY-IT**
1. In a class of 24, 9 students prefer football. What angle of a pie chart does football
   take?
2. One quarter of a class of 28 prefers the swimming pool. How many students is that?
*Answers: (1) 9/24 × 360° = 135°. (2) 7.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A magazine graph shows ice-cream sales "falling 90%", but its vertical axis starts at 10
instead of 0. Explain, in one or two sentences, why the picture can exaggerate the claim.
*Answer direction: with a truncated axis the visible bar lengths represent differences from
10, not from zero, so the shorter bar looks almost vanishing even when the true values are
still large — the shape amplifies the relative change.*
