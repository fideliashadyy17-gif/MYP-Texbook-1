# MYP Mathematics 2 — Spec-gap additions (Try-it checks + Stretch & Apply)

Same gap as MYP1: the book's "How the labels work" page (p10) lists Definition, Key idea,
Common error, ATL and Criterion-strip boxes, but the course spec also requires **Try-it
checks** and **Stretch & Apply (SaA)** boxes, of which the book has none. One of each per
chapter (11 chapters), ready to drop in.

**Placement convention**
- **Try-it check**: after the first worked example of the chapter's central section
  (section noted per chapter).
- **Stretch & Apply**: at the end of the concept sequence, just before the chapter's
  investigation/criterion task.

**Label-page text to add (p10, "How the labels work"):**
> Try-it checks ask one or two rapid questions the moment a method is first shown. They test
> whether the new move is safe before you carry on; answers are given, so you can correct
> yourself in seconds.
>
> Stretch & Apply boxes move the method one step into a new context. They are deliberately
> short, sit at the end of a concept sequence, and reward connecting the idea to something
> you have already seen.

## Chapter 1 · Ratios and equivalent ratios
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 1.2 (equivalent ratios); SaA before
"The lemonade stand".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Write 15 : 25 in simplest form.
2. A recipe mixes flour and water in the ratio 3 : 2. For 18 cups of flour, how many cups
   of water are needed?
*Answers: (1) 3 : 5. (2) 12.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A fruit punch mixes juice and soda in the ratio 2 : 3. A student mixes 4 cups of juice with
6 cups of soda and says the punch "tastes twice as strong because there is more of it".
Explain, in ratio language, why the taste does not change.
*Answer direction: 4 : 6 simplifies to 2 : 3 — an equivalent ratio; strength depends on the
ratio, not on the total amount.*

## Chapter 2 · Proportional reasoning
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 2.1 (recognising proportional
relationships); SaA before "A plan that fits".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Is the relationship y = 4x proportional? What is the constant of proportionality?
2. Five pens cost £3. At the same price, what do 8 pens cost?
*Answers: (1) Yes; k = 4. (2) £4.80.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A map drawn at scale 1 : 100 000 shows two towns 4 cm apart. What is the real distance? If
the map is then enlarged with scale factor 2 (every length doubles), what is the new scale?
*Answer: 4 km; 1 : 50 000.*

## Chapter 3 · Rates and unit rates
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 3.2 (unit prices and best buys); SaA
before "The unit-price trap".*

**TRY-IT**
1. 3 kg of apples cost £4.50. Find the unit price.
2. Which is the better buy: 500 g for £1.20, or 1 kg for £2.30?
*Answers: (1) £1.50 per kg. (2) 500 g costs £2.40 per kg equivalent, so the 1 kg pack at
£2.30 per kg is better.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Printer A prints 24 pages in 6 minutes; printer B prints 36 pages in 10 minutes. Which is
faster, and by how many pages per minute?
*Answer: A does 4 pages/min; B does 3.6 pages/min — A is faster by 0.4 pages/min.*

## Chapter 4 · Integers and operations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 4.2 (adding and subtracting
integers); SaA before "A mountain weather station".*

**TRY-IT**
1. (−7) + 12
2. (−5) − (−9)
*Answers: (1) 5. (2) 4.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A hot-air balloon is at 300 m. It rises 240 m, then descends 520 m. Using signed numbers for
each stage, what is its final height above the ground?
*Answer: +240 then −520; 300 + 240 − 520 = 20 m.*

## Chapter 5 · Algebraic expressions and equations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 5.3 (solving linear equations); SaA
before "Choosing a repair plan".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve 3x + 4 = 19.
2. Evaluate 2n − 5 when n = 6.
*Answers: (1) x = 5. (2) 7.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A repair plan charges a £10 call-out fee plus £15 per hour of work. Write an expression for
the cost C of h hours, then find h when the bill is £100.
*Answer: C = 10 + 15h; 15h = 90, so h = 6.*

## Chapter 6 · Numerical patterns and generalisation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 6.2 (the nth term); SaA before "The
lantern arch".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the nth term of the sequence 5, 8, 11, 14, …
2. Use your rule to find the 20th term.
*Answers: (1) 3n + 2. (2) 62.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A function machine is "in → multiply by 2 → subtract 3 → out". Which input gives the output
17? Write the whole machine as one expression.
*Answer: 2x − 3 = 17, so x = 10.*

## Chapter 7 · 2D geometry: angles, perimeter and area
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 7.1 (angle relationships); SaA before
"The courtyard budget".*

**TRY-IT**
1. The angles of a triangle are x°, (x + 25)° and (2x − 5)°. Find x.
2. Find the area of a circle of radius 7 cm, leaving your answer in terms of π.
*Answers: (1) 4x + 20° = 180°, so x = 40 (angles 40°, 65°, 75°). (2) 49π cm².*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Floor tiles are 20 cm squares. A corridor 4 m long is tiled edge to edge along its length.
How many tiles are needed, and what total area do they cover in m²?
*Answer: 4 m = 200 cm, so 10 tiles; 10 × 0.04 m² = 0.4 m².*

## Chapter 8 · 3D geometry: solids, surface area and volume
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 8.4 (volume of prisms and
cylinders); SaA before "The water tank".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the volume of a cuboid measuring 5 cm by 3 cm by 4 cm.
2. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, in terms of π.
*Answers: (1) 60 cm³. (2) 90π cm³.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A water tank 2 m by 1.5 m by 1.2 m is completely full. How many litres of water does it
contain? (1 m³ = 1000 L)
*Answer: 2 × 1.5 × 1.2 = 3.6 m³ = 3600 L.*

## Chapter 9 · Transformations and symmetry
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 9.1 (coordinates and
translations); SaA before "Symmetry and tessellation".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Translate the point (2, −3) by (−4, +5).
2. Reflect the point (1, 4) in the x-axis.
*Answers: (1) (−2, 2). (2) (1, −4).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A flag on a community banner is at (3, 2). It is translated by (5, −1), then its image is
reflected in the y-axis. Where is the flag finally?
*Answer: (3, 2) → (8, 1) → (−8, 1).*

## Chapter 10 · Probability and simulation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 10.2 (theoretical probability); SaA
before "Is the game fair?".*

**TRY-IT**
1. A bag holds 3 red and 5 blue balls. Find P(red).
2. Find P(not red).
*Answers: (1) 3/8. (2) 5/8 (the complement).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A two-digit code uses the digits 1–9 for each digit. How many different codes are
possible, and what is the probability that the two digits are the same?
*Answer: 9 × 9 = 81 codes; 9 of them (11, 22, …, 99), so 9/81 = 1/9.*

## Chapter 11 · Univariate data
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 11.1 (organising and summarising
data); SaA before "Reaction times".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the mean, median and range of 4, 6, 6, 8, 10.
2. What is the mode of the data?
*Answers: (1) Mean 6.8, median 6, range 6. (2) 6.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Five students' puzzle times (seconds) are 12, 15, 15, 18, 40. Which average best
represents a "typical" time, and why?
*Answer: the median (15) — the 40 s outlier pulls the mean up to 20, so the median
represents the typical performance better.*
