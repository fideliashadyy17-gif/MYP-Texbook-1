## Chapter 1 · Polynomials, Factor Theorem and Algebraic Fractions
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 1.3 (remainder and factor theorems);
SaA before the investigation.*

**TRY-IT**
Let f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6.
1. Find f(1).
2. What does f(1) = 0 tell you about x − 1?
*Answers: (1) 0. (2) x − 1 is a factor of f (factor theorem), so x = 1 is a root.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
An open box is made from a 12 cm square sheet by cutting squares of side x from the
corners; its volume is V = x(12 − 2x)². Show that V is a cubic polynomial in x, then find
V when x = 2.
*Answer: V = x(144 − 48x + 4x²) = 4x³ − 48x² + 144x; V(2) = 32 − 192 + 288 = 128 cm³.*

## Chapter 2 · Functions: Transformations, Composites and Inverses
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 2.3 (composite functions); SaA before
the investigation.*

**TRY-IT**
Let f(x) = 2x + 3 and g(x) = x².
1. Find f(g(x)).
2. Find g(f(x)).
*Answers: (1) 2x² + 3. (2) (2x + 3)² — the order changes the result.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
f(x) = 3x − 2. Find f⁻¹(x), then use the inverse (not inspection) to solve 3x − 2 = 17.
*Answer: f⁻¹(x) = (x + 2)/3; x = f⁻¹(17) = 19/3.*

## Chapter 3 · Exponential and Logarithmic Functions
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 3.1 (exponential equations); SaA
before "how many digits?".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve 2ˣ = 32.
2. Solve 5^(2x) = 125.
*Answers: (1) x = 5. (2) 2x = 3, so x = 1.5.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A population doubles every 40 years. Write a model for P (in initial-value units) after t
years, and find the annual growth factor to 2 decimal places.
*Answer: P = 2^(t/40); annual factor 2^(1/40) = e^(ln 2/40) ≈ 1.02.*

## Chapter 4 · Sequences and Series
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 4.3 (geometric sequences); SaA before
"infinite sums".*

**TRY-IT**
The geometric sequence 3, 6, 12, …
1. Find u₈.
2. Find the sum of the first 8 terms.
*Answers: (1) 3 × 2⁷ = 384. (2) S₈ = 3(2⁸ − 1) = 765.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A ball that bounces to ¾ of its previous height is dropped from 1 m. Find the total
distance it travels (down and up) before coming to rest.
*Answer: 1 + 2(0.75 + 0.75² + ⋯) = 1 + 2 × (0.75/0.25) = 7 m.*

## Chapter 5 · Quadratic Inequalities and Linear Programming
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 5.1 (quadratic inequalities in one
variable); SaA before "integer solutions and the vertex".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve x² − 5x + 6 < 0.
2. Solve x² + 2x − 8 ≥ 0.
*Answers: (1) 2 < x < 3. (2) x ≤ −4 or x ≥ 2.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A farmer grows wheat (profit £100 per tonne) and maize (profit £60 per tonne). She can
grow at most 30 tonnes in total, must grow at least 5 tonnes of maize, and can grow at
most 20 tonnes of wheat. What mix maximises profit?
*Answer: profit 100w + 60m over vertices (0, 5) → 300; (0, 30) → 1800; (20, 10) → 2600;
(20, 5) → 2300. Maximum £2600 at 20 t wheat, 10 t maize.*

## Chapter 6 · Circle Geometry and Geometric Proof
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 6.1 (angles in circles); SaA before
"circle theorems under transformation".*

**TRY-IT**
1. An angle at the circumference subtends an arc of 100°. Find the angle.
2. A cyclic quadrilateral has one angle of 72°. Find the opposite angle.
*Answers: (1) 50° (angle at centre is twice the angle at circumference). (2) 108°
(opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
AB is a diameter of a circle and C is on the circle. If ∠CAB = 34°, find ∠ABC.
*Answer: ∠ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so ∠ABC = 180° − 90° − 34° = 56°.*

## Chapter 7 · Sine and Cosine Rules; Trigonometry in Three Dimensions
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 7.1 (the sine rule); SaA before
"measuring the unmeasurable".*

**TRY-IT**
In a triangle, a = 8, A = 30° and B = 45°.
1. Find b.
2. Find C.
*Answers: (1) b = 8 sin 45°/sin 30° = 8√2 ≈ 11.3. (2) C = 105°.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A vertical tower is observed from two points 100 m apart on a straight horizontal line;
the angles of elevation are 30° and 50° (from the nearer point). Find the tower's height
to 1 decimal place.
*Answer: h = 100 tan 30° tan 50°/(tan 50° − tan 30°) ≈ 112.0 m.*

## Chapter 8 · Vectors and Matrices
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 8.1 (vector notation and
operations); SaA before section 8.6.*

**TRY-IT**
a = (3, −2) and b = (−1, 4).
1. Find a + b.
2. Find |a|.
*Answers: (1) (2, 2). (2) √13 ≈ 3.6.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A = (1 2; 3 4). Find A⁻¹ and verify that AA⁻¹ is the identity.
*Answer: det A = −2, so A⁻¹ = (−2 1; 1.5 −0.5); AA⁻¹ = (−2+3, 1−1; −6+6, 3−2) = (1 0; 0 1).*

## Chapter 9 · Statistics: Dispersion, Correlation and Regression
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 9.2 (standard deviation and grouped
data); SaA before "correlation, causation and reliability".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the mean of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
2. Find the standard deviation of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 (using the ÷ n convention), to 2 decimal
   places.
*Answers: (1) 6. (2) deviations −4, −2, 0, 2, 4; squared sum 40; 40/5 = 8; √8 ≈ 2.83.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A least-squares line ŷ = 4 + 0.8x has r = 0.6. Interpret r² in context, and state one
thing it cannot tell you.
*Answer: r² = 0.36 — the linear model explains 36% of the variation in y; it cannot
establish that x causes y (or that the relationship is linear beyond the data range).*

## Chapter 10 · Probability: Conditional Probability and Counting
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 10.2 (conditional probability); SaA
before "the birthday problem".*

**TRY-IT**
A bag holds 4 red and 6 blue balls; two are drawn without replacement.
1. Find P(first ball is red).
2. Find P(both balls are red).
*Answers: (1) 4/10. (2) (4/10)(3/9) = 2/15 ≈ 0.133.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
How many 4-digit codes can be made from the digits 1–9 with no digit repeated? How many
of them end in an even digit?
*Answer: 9 × 8 × 7 × 6 = 3024; codes ending in an even digit: 4 × 8 × 7 × 6 = 1344.*
