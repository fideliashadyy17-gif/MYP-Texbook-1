## Chapter 1 · Number Foundations: Exponents, Surds and Accuracy
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 1.2 (integer and rational
exponents); SaA before the Chapter Assessment.*

**TRY-IT**
1. Simplify x³ · x⁴.
2. Evaluate 8^(2/3).
*Answers: (1) x⁷. (2) (∛8)² = 4.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Express 5√3 as a single square root, and simplify (√3)² + (√5)².
*Answer: √75; 3 + 5 = 8.*

## Chapter 2 · Algebraic Expressions and Factorisation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 2.3 (quadratic factorisation); SaA
before section 2.6.*

**TRY-IT**
1. Factorise x² − 5x + 6.
2. Factorise x² − 16.
*Answers: (1) (x − 2)(x − 3). (2) (x − 4)(x + 4).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A product's profit is P = −x² + 12x − 20, where x is the price in pounds. For which prices
is the profit positive?
*Answer: P = −(x − 2)(x − 10) > 0 for 2 < x < 10.*

## Chapter 3 · Linear Equations, Inequalities and Systems
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 3.3 (systems: graphs and
substitution); SaA before the break-even investigation.*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve the system y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 7.
2. Solve 3x − 2 ≤ 10.
*Answers: (1) 2x + 1 = −x + 7 → x = 2, y = 5. (2) x ≤ 4.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A market stall has £50 of fixed costs and makes £3 profit per item sold. From which item
number is the total profit positive?
*Answer: 3n − 50 > 0 → n > 16⅔, so from the 17th item (16 items: −£2; 17 items: +£1).*

## Chapter 4 · Functions, Coordinates and Linear Models
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 4.3 (gradient and equations of
lines); SaA before "modelling a fair fare".*

**TRY-IT**
1. The line through (0, 5) and (2, 1): find its gradient and equation.
2. Find the midpoint of (1, 2) and (5, 6).
*Answers: (1) m = (1 − 5)/(2 − 0) = −2; y = −2x + 5. (2) (3, 4).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
What is the relationship between y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x − 4? Find the equation of the line
through (0, 2) that is perpendicular to y = 3x + 1.
*Answer: parallel (same gradient); perpendicular gradient −1/3, so y = −⅓x + 2.*

## Chapter 5 · Quadratic Expressions and Equations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 5.2 (solving by factorising); SaA
before "the discriminant detective".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve x² − 4x + 3 = 0 by factorising.
2. Find the discriminant of x² + 2x + 5, and say how many real solutions it has.
*Answers: (1) x = 1 or x = 3. (2) 2² − 4(1)(5) = −16 < 0, so no real solutions.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A ball's height (metres) after t seconds is h = 16t − 5t². When does it land, and what is
its maximum height?
*Answer: t(16 − 5t) = 0 → t = 3.2 s; vertex at t = 1.6 s, h = 16(1.6) − 5(1.6)² = 12.8 m.*

## Chapter 6 · Quadratic Functions and Graphical Reasoning
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 6.2 (vertex form and
transformations); SaA before "arches and trajectories".*

**TRY-IT**
1. State the vertex of y = (x − 2)² + 3.
2. Find the x-intercepts of y = x² − 4.
*Answers: (1) (2, 3). (2) x = 2 and x = −2.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A profit model is P = −(x − 6)² + 25, where x is the price. What price gives the maximum
profit, and what is that maximum?
*Answer: x = 6, with P = 25.*

## Chapter 7 · Patterns, Sequences and Generalisation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 7.3 (quadratic sequences and
differences); SaA before "figures that grow".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the nth term of 2, 5, 10, 17, …
2. Use your rule to find the 6th term.
*Answers: (1) n² + 1 (second differences are constant at 2). (2) 37.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Dot figures follow the square numbers 1, 4, 9, 16, … How many dots are in figure 15, and
how many more than figure 14?
*Answer: 15² = 225; 225 − 196 = 29 more.*

## Chapter 8 · Growth and Financial Mathematics
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 8.2 (compound interest and
multipliers); SaA before "doubling and halving".*

**TRY-IT**
1. £100 grows at 5% per year. Find its value after 2 years.
2. What single multiplier represents a 12% decrease?
*Answers: (1) 100 × 1.05² = £110.25. (2) 0.88.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A car worth £20 000 depreciates 10% per year. Find its value after 3 years (2 decimal
places where needed).
*Answer: 20 000 × 0.9³ = £14 580.*

## Chapter 9 · Congruence, Similarity and Transformations
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 9.3 (enlargement and scale
factors); SaA before "shadows and scale".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Enlarge the point (2, 3) from the origin with scale factor 3.
2. Lengths are multiplied by 4 in an enlargement. By what factor is area multiplied?
*Answers: (1) (6, 9). (2) 4² = 16.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Two similar storage boxes have edges 3 cm and 6 cm. If the larger holds 540 cm³, how much
does the smaller hold?
*Answer: linear ratio 1 : 2 → volume ratio 1 : 8, so 540 ÷ 8 = 67.5 cm³.*

## Chapter 10 · Pythagoras, Trigonometry and Measurement
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 10.3 (trigonometric ratios); SaA
before "measuring the inaccessible".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Compare sin 30° and cos 60°. What relationship do they suggest?
2. In a right-angled triangle the side opposite a 40° angle is 9. Find the hypotenuse to
   1 decimal place.
*Answers: (1) Both equal 0.5 — cos θ = sin(90° − θ). (2) 9/sin 40° = 9/0.6428 ≈ 14.0.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
From a lighthouse, one ship bears 090° at 2 km and another bears 180° at 3 km. Find the
angle at the lighthouse and the distance between the ships (1 decimal place).
*Answer: 90°; √(2² + 3²) = √13 ≈ 3.6 km.*

## Chapter 11 · Surface Area and Volume
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 11.1 (prisms and cylinders); SaA
before "the most efficient package".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the total surface area of a cuboid measuring 2 cm by 3 cm by 4 cm.
2. Find the total surface area of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, in terms of π.
*Answers: (1) 2(6 + 8 + 12) = 52 cm². (2) 2π(2²) + 2π(2)(5) = 28π cm².*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A cube of side 2 cm is enlarged with scale factor 2. What happens to its surface area and
its volume?
*Answer: surface area ×4 (24 → 96 cm²); volume ×8 (8 → 64 cm³).*

## Chapter 12 · Statistics: Distributions and Data
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 12.3 (cumulative frequency and box
plots); SaA before "is the school day too long?".*

**TRY-IT**
1. A box plot has Q1 = 10, median = 16, Q3 = 22. Find the IQR.
2. Using the fence rule (Q3 + 1.5 × IQR), is 40 an outlier?
*Answers: (1) 12. (2) Upper fence = 22 + 18 = 40; 40 is not above the fence, so it is not
an outlier (it sits exactly on it).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Two classes both average 70 on a test; Class A has sd 8, Class B has sd 2. Which class is
more consistent, and what should a teacher plan differently for the other class?
*Answer: Class B is more consistent (scores cluster tightly on the mean); Class A's wider
spread suggests mixed readiness, so differentiated support is needed.*

## Chapter 13 · Probability: Combined Events
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 13.3 (tree diagrams); SaA before "is
the game fair?".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Two fair coins are tossed. Find P(exactly one head).
2. Two tiles are drawn without replacement from 3 red and 2 blue. Find P(both red).
*Answers: (1) 2 of 4 outcomes, so 1/2. (2) (3/5)(2/4) = 3/10.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A spinner has 4 equal sectors (red, green, blue, yellow). It is spun twice. Find P(same
colour both times).
*Answer: 4 of 16 outcomes (RR, GG, BB, YY), so 4/16 = 1/4.*
