## Chapter 1 · The real number system
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 1.4 (working with surds); SaA before
"Chasing √2 with fractions".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Simplify √50.
2. Simplify √12 + √27.
*Answers: (1) 5√2. (2) 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Is √7 rational or irrational? Explain using the definition of a rational number.
*Answer direction: irrational. If √7 = p/q in lowest terms, then p² = 7q², so 7 divides p,
hence 7 divides q — contradicting "lowest terms" (same structure as the √2 proof).*

## Chapter 2 · Powers, roots and scientific notation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 2.1 (exponent laws); SaA before "A
scale model of the solar system".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Simplify 2⁵ × 2³.
2. Simplify (3²)⁴.
*Answers: (1) 2⁸. (2) 3⁸.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Write 0.0047 and 470 000 000 in standard form. Then decide which is larger: 4.7 × 10⁻³ or
47 × 10⁻⁴ — and explain why the comparison is not obvious from the forms.
*Answer: 4.7 × 10⁻³ and 4.7 × 10⁸; they are equal (47 × 10⁻⁴ = 4.7 × 10⁻³) — standard form
is only unique when the front digit is 1–9.*

## Chapter 3 · Functions, expressions and algebraic reasoning
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 3.3 (expanding and factorising); SaA
before "The matchstick triangles".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Expand 3(2x − 5).
2. Factorise x² + 7x + 12.
*Answers: (1) 6x − 15. (2) (x + 3)(x + 4).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A rectangle has area x² − 9 and one side x − 3. Find the other side.
*Answer: (x² − 9)/(x − 3) = (x − 3)(x + 3)/(x − 3) = x + 3.*

## Chapter 4 · Linear relationships and graphs
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 4.2 (gradient as a rate of
change); SaA before "A library of things".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the gradient of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8).
2. Write the equation of the line with gradient 2 and y-intercept −3.
*Answers: (1) (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 2. (2) y = 2x − 3.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A taxi charges a £3 flag-fall plus £2 per kilometre. Write the cost rule for a journey of
d km, and say what the y-intercept and the gradient each mean in the situation.
*Answer: C = 3 + 2d; intercept = the charge before any distance is travelled, gradient =
the rate per km.*

## Chapter 5 · Equations, inequalities and systems
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 5.1 (solving linear equations); SaA
before "The three weighings".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Solve 2x − 3 > 5.
2. Solve the system y = x + 1 and y = 2x − 1.
*Answers: (1) x > 4. (2) x = 2, y = 3.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A café sells coffee at £2 and tea at £1.50. On one day it sold 40 drinks and took in £75.
How many of each did it sell?
*Answer: c + t = 40 and 2c + 1.5t = 75 → c = 30, t = 10 (check: 60 + 15 = 75).*

## Chapter 6 · Transformations, congruence and symmetry
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 6.3 (rotating about a point); SaA
before "The angle that fits".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Rotate the point (2, 1) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin.
2. Translate (0, 0) by (3, −4), then reflect the image in the x-axis.
*Answers: (1) (−1, 2). (2) (3, −4) → (3, 4).*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A triangle has vertices (1, 1), (4, 1), (1, 4). A second triangle has vertices (−1, −1),
(−4, −1), (−1, −4). Name a transformation that maps the first onto the second, and say
whether the image is congruent to the original.
*Answer: rotation of 180° about the origin (equivalently, reflection in the origin); yes —
rotations preserve lengths and angles.*

## Chapter 7 · Similarity, proof and trigonometry
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 7.3 (Pythagoras' theorem); SaA before
"The shadow rule".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with legs 6 and 8.
2. In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite θ is 5 and the hypotenuse is 13. Find
   sin θ and the angle θ to 1 decimal place.
*Answers: (1) 10. (2) sin θ = 5/13 ≈ 0.385; θ ≈ 22.6°.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A ladder 5 m long leans against a wall, its foot 1.4 m from the wall. How high up the wall
does it reach? (1 decimal place)
*Answer: √(5² − 1.4²) = √23.04 ≈ 4.8 m.*

## Chapter 8 · Three-dimensional solids and measurement
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 8.4 (volume of prisms and
cylinders); SaA before "The rainwater tank".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Find the volume of a cone of radius 4 cm and height 9 cm, in terms of π.
2. Find the volume of a sphere of radius 3 cm, in terms of π.
*Answers: (1) ⅓π(16)(9) = 48π cm³. (2) 4/3 π(27) = 36π cm³.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A rainwater tank 1 m by 0.8 m by 0.6 m reaches 60% of its capacity after a storm. How many
litres of water did it collect?
*Answer: capacity 0.48 m³ = 480 L; 60% of 480 = 288 L.*

## Chapter 9 · Bivariate data, correlation and causation
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 9.2 (measuring linear
association); SaA before "The screen-time claim".*

**TRY-IT**
1. A correlation of r = 0.9 between study time and test score — describe the association.
2. If r = 0 for two variables, what can and cannot be concluded?
*Answers: (1) Strong positive linear association. (2) There is no linear association; a
non-linear relationship may still exist, and correlation never establishes cause.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
Ice-cream sales and sunburn cases both rise over the summer. A headline claims ice-cream
"causes" sunburn. Explain the flaw in the claim and name the hidden factor that explains
both.
*Answer: confounding — warmer weather (more outdoor time) raises both; association is not
causation.*

## Chapter 10 · Probability, sampling and evidence
*Trial placement: after the first worked example in 10.2 (compound events); SaA before
"The screening test".*

**TRY-IT**
1. Two fair dice are rolled. Find P(both even).
2. Find P(the sum is 7).
*Answers: (1) (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4. (2) 6 of 36 outcomes, so 1/6.*

**STRETCH & APPLY**
A screening test is 95% accurate. In a population where 1% have the condition, estimate the
fraction of positive results that are false positives, using a population of 10 000.
*Answer: 100 have it → 95 true positives; 9 900 are healthy → about 495 false positives;
of 590 positives, about 495/590 ≈ 84% are false positives (base-rate effect).*
